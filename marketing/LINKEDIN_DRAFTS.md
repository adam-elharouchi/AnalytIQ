## Post 3 — Professor time-savings insight

**Status: Ready to personalize.** This is a thesis/observation post, not a data or testimonial post — it does not require a testimonial or a specific number, and none is used. It states the problem AnalytIQ is built to solve, in Adam's own reasoning, without claiming a measured result.

> Every professor I've talked to who teaches anything code- or data-adjacent has some version of the same week: teach the material, then go build practice problems that actually match what you just taught, then grade them one by one.
>
> The generic platforms don't help with that — Datacamp and Coursera teach *their* curriculum, in their notation, with their examples. A professor can assign it, but it's not what they taught in week 4. So the choice becomes: build it yourself, or leave the gap open and hope students bridge it on their own.
>
> That's the actual problem AnalytIQ is built around. Upload the syllabus you already have, and the exercises come out aligned to it — your notation, your logic, your datasets — auto-graded, in about the time it takes to get coffee. The professor's time savings isn't a feature. It's the whole point.
>
> I'd rather build the tool that gives that week back than the platform that adds one more thing to grade.
>
> #EdTech #HigherEd #ProductBuilding

Every claim in this draft is a description of the product mechanism (upload → aligned, auto-graded exercises, under 60 seconds) or Adam's own stated reasoning — no usage numbers, no specific hours-saved figure, nothing that requires a testimonial.
